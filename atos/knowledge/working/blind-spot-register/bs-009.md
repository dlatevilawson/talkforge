## What could fail?

Motion, lighting, sound, haptics, and emotional pacing could become theatrical branding that delays action, disguises latency, simulates intelligence, or manipulates attention instead of preparing the member.

## Unintended consequences

- Machine activation becomes a compulsory animation before training.
- “Thinking” behavior falsely implies human cognition or progress.
- Personality differences reduce predictability and accessibility.
- Sound and haptics create pressure, embarrassment, or sensory fatigue.
- Premium behavior performs luxury rather than improving comprehension.
- Recovery sequences prioritize visual polish over restoring the member’s exact state.

## Trust impact

High. Fake progress, unexplained waiting, excessive animation, or state ambiguity makes TalkForge feel less reliable precisely when members are vulnerable.

## Autonomy impact

High if rituals delay pause, decline, skip, exit, or recovery actions.

## Complexity impact

High. Cross-platform motion, audio, haptics, reduced-motion parity, and interruption recovery require disciplined implementation and testing.

## Mission alignment

Aligned only when behavior transforms hesitation into readiness while preserving dignity, accessibility, and control.

## Mitigation / decision

1. Controls become available when the system is ready, never when animation finishes.
2. Waiting behavior reports known state; it never invents progress.
3. Every behavioral cue has a text, structural, or assistive equivalent.
4. Sound and haptics default to restraint and respect device preferences.
5. Recovery restores function and state before presenting polish.
6. Behavioral personality may vary by Machine, but action placement and system semantics remain consistent.
7. Test emotional pacing with members; do not infer emotional outcomes from designer intent alone.
