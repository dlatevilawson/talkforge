## What could fail?

A visitor can clear cookies, change browsers or devices, rotate network identity, or automate requests to obtain more than one anonymous preview. A signed browser cookie proves possession of that browser-bound secret; it does not prove a durable person identity.

## Unintended consequences

- Repeated unpaid Realtime sessions can create material model and voice cost.
- Aggressive device fingerprinting can create privacy and trust harm without reliably proving personhood.
- Shared browsers can incorrectly treat another person as having used the preview.
- Treating the preview as account identity can accidentally revive the retired `guest_*` architecture.
- “Maybe later” can become an accidental free-continuation bypass if the composer/input is re-enabled after the auth prompt closes.

## Trust impact

High if TalkForge claims “one per person” when it can enforce only best-effort browser continuity, or if it uses invasive fingerprinting without explicit governance.

## Autonomy impact

Low under a signed cookie and transparent post-session auth choice. High if the product silently fingerprints people or forces authentication before they receive the promised preview.

## Complexity impact

Moderate. Server-authoritative session minting, Realtime authorization, rate limits, concurrent-session controls, spend ceilings, and abuse monitoring are required. A new guest identity system is neither required nor authorized.

## Mission alignment

One private practice session before commitment demonstrates the communication gym directly. Economic controls must protect sustainability without misrepresenting identity certainty or interrupting an active session.

## Mitigation / decision

Decision 060 accepts a best-effort browser-bound limit: signed HttpOnly cookie + server anonymous session, 14-day claim continuity where useful, origin and rate controls, concurrent-session controls, and server-authoritative Realtime/economic guards. The preview does not consume the authenticated Free member’s three-session calendar-month allowance. The composer/input is disabled while the post-session auth prompt is visible and remains disabled after “Maybe later”; the deferred state offers only **“Ready to practice again? Create an account for 3 free sessions every month.”** and an auth-linked **“Get started.”** Do not claim provable one-person enforcement, use client entitlement claims, revive `guest_*`, authorize cross-device archive recovery, or allow the tertiary action to mint another turn/session.
