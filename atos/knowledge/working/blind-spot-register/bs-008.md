## What could fail?

The design system could optimize for visual uniformity, token completeness, or premium appearance instead of helping members train sooner. It could also freeze experimentation prematurely, turn the physical Gym metaphor into decorative skeuomorphism, or make accessible fallback experiences feel secondary.

## Unintended consequences

- Designers conform to components even when the member’s context requires a clearer pattern.
- Machine identity becomes a rainbow of visual effects rather than recognizable equipment.
- Motion, lighting, haptics, or sound delay the primary action.
- Progress displays fabricated precision because quantified interfaces appear more credible.
- Maintaining the system becomes a substitute for improving the product.

## Trust impact

High. Beautiful but unexplained recommendations, inaccessible state cues, or unsupported progress claims make TalkForge feel manipulative rather than coach-like.

## Autonomy impact

High if the system removes redirection in pursuit of a single polished path. One recommendation must preserve “Not today,” alternatives, and Gym exploration.

## Complexity impact

Medium to high. Tokens and primitives should reduce duplication; a large component framework would increase implementation and governance burden.

## Mission alignment

Aligned only when the system makes preparation clearer, safer, faster, and more coherent.

## Mitigation / decision

1. Beauty must reduce uncertainty or be removed.
2. Start with a small semantic token and component set grounded in existing surfaces.
3. No motion, sound, lighting, or haptic sequence may delay interaction.
4. Every state must remain understandable without color, animation, sound, or vibration.
5. Metrics require validated operational definitions, provenance, limitations, and dignity review.
6. Review the system after real Coach Homepage and Training Room use; do not treat v1.0 as permanently complete.
