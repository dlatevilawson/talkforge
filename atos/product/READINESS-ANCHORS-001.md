## Purpose

This contract expands the five readiness signals into 25 behavioral
anchors: five internal evidence levels for each signal. It gives a human
reviewer a concrete starting point for deciding what TalkForge means by
demonstrated readiness while preserving the parent contract's boundaries.

The examples are illustrations, not scripts or keyword matches. A reviewer must
judge the member's behavior in context and cite evidence from the current
session.

## Interpretation rules

1. Assign the highest level whose required behavior is fully supported and whose
   disqualifying behavior is absent. A disqualifier means “this anchor is not
   established,” not a judgment about the member.
2. Evidence strength and level are separate. A plausible level with thin
   evidence is `limited`; it cannot affect an overall band or trend. A signal
   with `insufficient` evidence is null, never level 0.
3. `Sufficient` evidence normally requires a test-and-response sequence or two
   distinct supporting references. The cell-specific minimum may be stricter.
4. Level 0 requires observed breakdown plus an opportunity to respond or
   recover. Silence, a short session, an accent, grammar, vocal style, or a
   delivery metric alone can never establish it.
5. Level 4 requires meaningful pressure or a member-created test of the signal.
   Smooth behavior in an untested session is not enough.
6. Delivery measures may corroborate behavior but never determine a readiness
   level by themselves.
7. No anchor permits personality inference, cross-member comparison, or a
   prediction of real-world success.

## Purpose — anchoring to the conversation's goal

| Level | Required behavior | Disqualifying behavior | Minimum evidence | Positive example | Counterexample |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Needs rebuilding** | The member observably abandons, contradicts, or completely avoids the stated goal and does not recover after an opportunity to do so. | A recognizable goal remains active, or the member returns to it even imperfectly. | A stated or clearly established goal, at least one derailment or avoidance event, and a later opportunity to return that was not used. | After saying the aim is to request a deadline change, the member argues about an unrelated past slight through the close and never makes the request. | The member drifts into the past slight, then says, “That is separate; I still need to ask for Friday.” This is not level 0. |
| **1 — Early practice** | The member attempts to hold a goal but loses it at the first meaningful friction, backtracks, or concedes merely to end discomfort. | The goal survives friction, or the member makes a functional return to it. | A goal attempt, one friction event, observed loss of the goal, and no clean return before the exchange ends. | The member opens with a clear boundary, then withdraws it immediately after one objection and ends without an ask. | The member softens the wording after an objection but keeps the same boundary. Adaptation is not goal loss. |
| **2 — Developing** | The goal is present and functional in straightforward turns but drifts under complexity; the member makes a partial or effortful return. | The goal remains consistently operative through friction, or no goal was testable. | At least three goal-relevant turns, one complication, one drift, and evidence of a partial return. | The member states the desired outcome, follows a tangent under pushback, then returns to the topic but closes without a specific ask. | A long answer that continues to build toward a clear ask is not drift merely because it is long. |
| **3 — Demonstrated** | The member states or makes the goal clear, uses it to resist tangents, and returns cleanly after a minor wobble, ending with a clear outcome or ask when the scenario calls for one. | A major goal loss remains unresolved, or the goal is never exposed to moderate friction. | At least four relevant turns including moderate friction, with evidence of anchoring or recovery and a coherent close. | “I understand the budget concern. My request is still a two-week extension; here is what it protects.” | Repeating the same ask without acknowledging new constraints shows persistence, but not necessarily demonstrated Purpose in context. |
| **4 — Sustained in practice** | The goal organizes the member's choices through the hardest pressure applied; the member can name tradeoffs, decline side arguments, and sharpen the close without becoming rigid. | The hardest pressure was absent, the member only repeats a memorized ask, or a late derailment changes the goal without deliberate choice. | At least four relevant turns, a high-pressure test or member-created sharpness, and two distinct references showing the goal held before and after that test. | Under an ultimatum, the member names what can change, what cannot, and closes with a precise next step tied to the original objective. | Winning the exchange or sounding forceful is not evidence that Purpose was sustained. |

## Perspective — accounting for the listener's world

| Level | Required behavior | Disqualifying behavior | Minimum evidence | Positive example | Counterexample |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Needs rebuilding** | The member repeatedly argues past an explicit constraint, dismisses the counterpart's stated stakes, or treats the other person as having no relevant perspective. | Any genuine attempt to understand or incorporate the defined counterpart's position. | A defined counterpart, an explicit constraint or viewpoint, repeated opportunity to engage it, and observed dismissal or non-engagement. | After the counterpart says staffing is the constraint twice, the member continues accusing them of not caring and never addresses staffing. | Disagreeing with the staffing decision while accurately naming the constraint is not level 0. |
| **1 — Early practice** | The member offers a token acknowledgment but abandons the listener's perspective at the first objection, substitutes mind-reading, or uses a question only to return to a fixed script. | The member incorporates any material part of the answer into the next move. | One acknowledgment or question, one counterpart response, and evidence that the response was ignored or overwritten. | “I know you're busy,” followed by the same demand after the counterpart explains a concrete deadline conflict. | “I hear the deadline conflict; would a smaller first delivery help?” incorporates the response and exceeds level 1. |
| **2 — Developing** | The member recognizes the listener's stakes in calm moments and asks at least one genuine question, but integration becomes incomplete or inconsistent under friction. | The listener's constraints consistently shape the member's framing through moderate friction. | At least three relevant turns, a genuine perspective-taking move, and one instance of incomplete integration under complication. | The member asks what risk worries the listener, reflects the answer, but later falls back to a generic pitch when challenged. | Warmth alone, without engaging the listener's actual stakes, does not establish level 2. |
| **3 — Demonstrated** | The member consistently names or elicits the listener's stakes, acknowledges constraints without forced agreement, and adapts the message around that information. | The perspective is assumed rather than checked, or it disappears when moderate pressure arrives. | At least four relevant turns including a question or accurate acknowledgment, a counterpart response, and an adapted follow-up under moderate friction. | “If predictability is the concern, let me show the two safeguards before I ask you to decide.” | Agreeing with everything to reduce tension is compliance, not demonstrated Perspective. |
| **4 — Sustained in practice** | Through the hardest pressure, the member can anticipate or steelman a serious objection, test that understanding, and integrate new information while preserving their own purpose. | The member caricatures the objection, claims unverifiable knowledge of motives, or faces no meaningful competing stake. | At least four relevant turns, high pressure or member-created steelmanning, and evidence before and after new information is introduced. | “Your strongest reason to say no is the launch risk—am I hearing that right? If so, here is a staged option that protects it.” | Correctly guessing an objection once, without checking or using it, is not level 4. |

## Composure — regulation under observable friction

**Null default:** If no friction, hard question, interruption, or comparable test
occurred, Composure is “Not measured in this session.”

| Level | Required behavior | Disqualifying behavior | Minimum evidence | Positive example | Counterexample |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Needs rebuilding** | Under observable friction, the member freezes, becomes hostile, completely avoids the exchange, or becomes incoherent and cannot resume after a clear recovery opportunity. | Any functional recovery, or a breakdown inferred only from tone, silence, fillers, accent, or pace. | A documented friction event, observable breakdown, and a later recovery opportunity that was not used. | After a hard objection, the member attacks the counterpart, abandons the scenario, and cannot restate the issue when invited to reset. | A long pause followed by a direct answer is regulation, not breakdown. |
| **1 — Early practice** | Regulation is attempted but collapses at the first friction: defensiveness, filler flooding, rushed concession, or avoidance persists without a functional recovery. | A partial recovery that restores the exchange, or no friction test. | One clear friction event, behavior change tied to that event, and continued instability through the next relevant turn. | The member answers calmly until challenged, then talks over the counterpart, retracts the point, and remains unable to continue it. | More fillers than usual do not establish level 1 unless they accompany an observed loss of regulation under pressure. |
| **2 — Developing** | A visible wobble occurs under pressure, but the member makes a partial or effortful recovery and continues the exchange. | No recovery at all, or consistently steady regulation through moderate friction. | At least one friction event, one observable wobble, and one recovery attempt with partial effectiveness. | The member rushes the first rebuttal, pauses when prompted, then restates the point more clearly but remains somewhat defensive. | Asking for a moment to think is not itself a wobble; it may support stronger Composure. |
| **3 — Demonstrated** | The member remains functional through moderate friction, can acknowledge difficulty, and recovers cleanly from a minor stumble without abandoning the goal. | A major unresolved loss of regulation, or no meaningful friction. | At least one moderate friction event and two references showing regulation or a clean recovery across the event. | “That question caught me off guard. Give me a second.” The member pauses, answers directly, and continues at a steady pace. | Sounding calm while evading the hard question does not establish demonstrated Composure. |
| **4 — Sustained in practice** | Regulation holds through the hardest moments; deliberate pacing, acknowledgment, and recovery allow pressure to sharpen rather than degrade the response. | Pressure was light, the response was merely rehearsed, or stability depended on avoiding the contested issue. | A high-pressure event or equivalent member-created test, plus evidence immediately before, during, and after it. | After an interruption and a personal challenge, the member pauses, names the concern without retaliation, and delivers a clearer answer. | A naturally slow voice is not level 4 without evidence of regulation under actual pressure. |

## Message — clarity and structure of the takeaway

| Level | Required behavior | Disqualifying behavior | Minimum evidence | Positive example | Counterexample |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Needs rebuilding** | Despite enough speaking opportunity, no coherent takeaway can be identified because the member contradicts, abandons, or fragments the central point and cannot repair it. | A listener could state a stable core point, even if the delivery is long or rough. | At least two substantive topic turns, a request or opportunity to clarify, and evidence that no stable takeaway emerged. | The member gives three incompatible answers to what they want and cannot choose or connect them when asked to summarize. | An unconventional structure with one repeatable conclusion is not level 0. |
| **1 — Early practice** | A core point is attempted but becomes unrecoverable through rambling, abstraction, backtracking, or the first follow-up; the ending does not land. | The core point remains repeatable or is functionally restored after clarification. | A point attempt, one clarification or follow-up, and evidence that the point becomes less clear or is abandoned. | The member begins with a request, adds unrelated reasons, reverses the request, and trails off when asked what matters most. | One verbal stumble followed by a clear restatement does not establish level 1. |
| **2 — Developing** | A recognizable core point and some structure are present, but ordering, concreteness, or the ending becomes inconsistent under complexity. | The takeaway stays clear and well-supported through moderate follow-up. | At least three relevant turns, an identifiable point, and one observed structural weakness or partial repair. | The member gives the point and an example, but after a follow-up adds several abstractions and closes without reconnecting them. | Brevity alone is not clarity; a short answer with no usable point does not automatically reach level 2. |
| **3 — Demonstrated** | A listener could repeat the core point; the member uses a coherent order, concrete support, and a clean ending, preserving clarity through moderate follow-up. | A material contradiction remains, the close is missing when required, or the message was never tested beyond a prepared opening. | At least three relevant turns including moderate follow-up, with distinct evidence for the core point and its supporting structure or close. | “My recommendation is to delay one week. It protects testing, and the failed checkout is the reason. I need approval today.” | Sophisticated vocabulary without a repeatable takeaway is not demonstrated Message. |
| **4 — Sustained in practice** | The message remains clear through the hardest questions; the member adjusts detail or structure to the listener while making the takeaway sharper, not merely shorter. | The hardest follow-up exposes contradiction, or clarity depends on repeating a memorized line unchanged. | A high-pressure follow-up or member-created test, plus evidence that the core point and structure remain coherent before and after it. | When challenged on cost, the member reorganizes the explanation around tradeoffs and ends with the same precise decision request. | Persuading the counterpart is an outcome and cannot by itself establish level 4. |

## Adaptability — tactical adjustment after a shift

**Null default:** If the session introduced no twist, objection, new information,
or failed first approach, Adaptability is “Not measured in this session.”

| Level | Required behavior | Disqualifying behavior | Minimum evidence | Positive example | Counterexample |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Needs rebuilding** | The member ignores a material shift, freezes, repeats a failed approach without change, or capitulates solely to end pressure, and cannot try another move when invited. | Any meaningful second tactic or clarifying move, even if imperfect. | A clear shift or failed approach, an opportunity to adjust, and repeated non-adjustment or complete avoidance. | After learning the decision-maker changed, the member repeats the same pitch to the wrong audience and declines to reframe when prompted. | Holding a sound boundary after new pressure is not rigidity if the tactic still engages the new information. |
| **1 — Early practice** | The member notices the shift but makes only a superficial change, repeats the same logic in new words, or loses the goal after one unsuccessful adjustment. | The second move materially uses the new information. | A shift, an initial approach, and a second move shown to be essentially unchanged or abandoned. | The counterpart raises a timing concern; the member says “I understand” and repeats the original pitch with no timing adjustment. | Changing the proposal from a full launch to a pilot is a material pivot, not level 1. |
| **2 — Developing** | The member tries a genuine second framing, question, or tactic, but integration is incomplete, late, or uneven. | The new information is consistently integrated while the goal remains intact through moderate pressure. | A defined shift, evidence of the first tactic, and one materially different follow-up with a visible limitation. | The member asks what would make a pilot acceptable and proposes one, but omits the constraint the counterpart just named. | A failed pivot can still demonstrate Adaptability; success is not required. |
| **3 — Demonstrated** | The member recognizes a moderate shift, incorporates it, and changes tactic or framing while preserving the underlying goal. | The adjustment is cosmetic, abandons the goal without deliberate choice, or was never tested by a real shift. | At least one moderate shift and two linked references showing the before tactic, new information, and materially adapted response. | After learning budget is fixed, the member changes from asking for money to asking for scope and timing tradeoffs. | Agreeing immediately to everything is compliance, not demonstrated Adaptability. |
| **4 — Sustained in practice** | Across the hardest or multiple shifts, the member treats failed attempts and new information as input, selects a meaningfully different tactic, and keeps purpose without becoming rigid. | Only one light shift occurred, the member cycles randomly through tactics, or the judgment relies on whether the counterpart accepted the move. | A high-pressure or multi-shift sequence with evidence of the original tactic, the shift or failure, the selected adjustment, and continued goal alignment. | After two objections reveal different risks, the member drops the generic pitch, asks which risk controls the decision, and builds a new staged proposal around the answer. | Fast improvisation without incorporating the actual new information is not level 4. |

## Version-change checklist

Before authorizing any later version, review each changed cell for:

- **Truth:** Does this describe the communication behavior TalkForge should
  recognize?
- **Separation:** Is the cell distinguishable from the levels directly above
  and below it?
- **Evidence:** Is the minimum strong enough to prevent inference from vibes?
- **Fairness:** Could accent, dialect, disability, personality, or speaking style
  be mistaken for the behavior?
- **Pressure:** Does level 4 require a real test without rewarding aggression or
  performance theater?
- **Language:** Do the examples coach behavior without grading human worth?

The Founder approved these anchors without amendment on 2026-09-23. Any change
to an anchor requires a new version; historical shadow assessments retain the
rubric version under which they were generated.
