## Principle

> **Every interaction should reduce unnecessary cognitive load so the member can focus on becoming more prepared.**

---

## Why this is invisible infrastructure

Constitution, Living Profile, Readiness, Homepage, Coach, Pedagogy, and Craft all serve this principle:

| Mechanism | Load removed |
|---|---|
| One mission above the fold | Choice overload |
| Readiness chooses what matters | Possibility overload |
| Pedagogy varies format | Fatigue from sameness |
| Anchoring great lines | Memory burden |
| Hold that thought | Interruption anxiety |
| Second opinions | Fear of missing a better choice |
| Evidence / provenance | Uncertainty |
| Adaptive coaching | Decision fatigue |

These are not isolated features. They solve one problem: **unnecessary cognitive load**.

---

## Dual test (with Craft Law #001)

| Instrument | Question |
|---|---|
| **CRAFT-LAW-001** | Does this feel more human, trustworthy, or courageous? |
| **DES-001** | Does this make thinking easier? |

When both answers are **yes**, we are likely building the right thing.  
When either is **no**, redesign.

---

## Guardrail

Reducing cognitive load must not reduce **autonomy** or **dignity** (CONST Article IX · Purpose Autonomy). Simplification that removes the member’s ability to redirect is over-narrowing — fail DES-001’s purpose (“focus on becoming more prepared”), not merely fewer clicks.
